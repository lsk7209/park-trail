What this estimate does—and does not—tell you

This calculator combines the distance and elevation gain you enter with a selected group pace and an optional planning buffer. The result is an estimated moving-time starting point, not a prediction of total time away from the car. Stops for views, children, navigation, photos, restrooms, shuttles, parking, weather changes, or a turn-around decision can add time without changing the route's distance or gain.

A short route can still take longer than the estimate when its surface, heat, altitude, snow, smoke, crowding, footing, or group needs differ from the simple inputs above. Likewise, a lower number does not mean a route is open, safe, accessible, suitable for a stroller, or appropriate for every member of a group. Use the result to compare options and to leave a time margin, not to compress a park-day plan.

How the calculation is framed

The tool starts with miles divided by the pace you choose, then adds a modest elevation-gain term and multiplies it by the selected adjustment. “Relaxed,” “Average,” and “Fit” are planning bands rather than fitness ratings. The kids-or-stroller and heavy-pack options are deliberately broad buffers; they do not measure surface width, cross-slope, fatigue, medical needs, weather exposure, or the experience of a specific person.

For that reason, enter the best stable route figures you can verify, read the result as a range-minded planning aid, and choose a backup or turn-around point before the outing becomes difficult to reverse. If the group's slowest or least flexible participant needs more margin, plan from that person's needs instead of from the calculator's fastest scenario.

Official route check before use

Gradient Trail's route cards and park snapshots are starting context. The National Park Service is the controlling source for same-day park alerts, closures, roads, weather, access notices, facilities, and other conditions that can change a plan. Open the official park page for your destination before leaving, then revisit it if weather, access, or group needs change.

When an official source and this estimate point in different directions, use the official information and change the plan. Call or contact the park when a route decision depends on mobility access, a closure, a shuttle, a facility, a permit, or an emergency concern.
